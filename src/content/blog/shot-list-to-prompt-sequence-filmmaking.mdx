## How do you convert a traditional film shot list into an AI prompt sequence?

**To convert a traditional film shot list into an AI prompt sequence**, map each row of your director's shot list (Scene, Shot Number, Lens, Framing, Character Action) into an explicit prompt formula: `[Framing + Camera Movement] + [Subject Action] + [Lighting & Film Stock Anchor]`. Numbering each prompt in sequential order and loading them into an automated browser queue allows you to generate complete film animatic frames directly from your production schedule.

By automating this process, pre-production teams can visualize a 30-shot commercial or short film sequence in a single afternoon.

---

## 1. The Shot List Mapping Formula

In traditional pre-production, a director or cinematographer creates a shot list table. Here is how standard cinematography columns translate directly into generative AI prompt syntax:

| Shot List Field | Film Specification | AI Prompt Translation Element |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Shot Type** | Wide / Establishing | `Wide angle establishing shot, deep focus, panoramic framing` |
| **Camera Movement** | Dolly / Tracking | `Low-angle tracking shot, subtle motion blur on foreground cobblestones` |
| **Lens Focal Length** | 85mm Portrait Lens | `85mm prime lens, shallow depth of field, creamy bokeh background` |
| **Lighting Setup** | Golden Hour / Rim Light | `Warm golden hour directional sidelight, subtle rim lighting on shoulders` |
| **Film Stock** | Kodak Vision3 500T | `Cinematic 35mm film grain, muted Kodak Vision3 color grading` |

## 2. Walkthrough: Converting a 3-Shot Scene

Consider a screenplay scene: *A private investigator discovers a break-in at his office at dusk.*

### Shot 1: Exterior Office Establishing (Wide)
- **Director's Note:** Establish the decrepit brownstone building and stormy twilight.
- **Automated Prompt:**
  ```text
  1. Wide establishing exterior shot, 1940s brick brownstone building at stormy twilight, rain puddles reflecting streetlamps, second-story window illuminated with yellow light, 35mm film grain
  ```

### Shot 2: Interior Doorway Breach (Medium)
- **Director's Note:** Reveal the splintered door lock and detective's hand drawing a revolver.
- **Automated Prompt:**
  ```text
  2. Medium shot from inside dim hallway, silhouette of detective in fedora pushing open a splintered wooden office door, right hand drawing a snub-nosed revolver, harsh yellow streetlight spill
  ```

### Shot 3: Over-the-Shoulder Discovery (Close Action)
- **Director's Note:** Focus on the ransacked filing cabinets through the detective's perspective.
- **Automated Prompt:**
  ```text
  3. Over-the-shoulder medium close-up behind detective in trenchcoat, looking into ransacked office with papers strewn across mahogany desk, tilted blinds casting geometric shadows, 35mm grain
  ```

## 3. Automating the Sequence with Flowmator

Once your shot list is translated into prompt text:
1. Export the list to a single CSV file with columns `Index`, `Shot`, and `Prompt`.
2. Load the CSV into Flowmator's side panel using the **Upload CSV** button.
3. Select **Safe Sequence Mode** to guarantee that Shot 2 does not begin generating until Shot 1's image tile is confirmed on the Google Flow canvas.
4. Enable **Auto-Download Images** so each rendered frame is saved directly to your project's media bin with chronological filename indexing (`Flowmator_Date_1.png`, `Flowmator_Date_2.png`).
