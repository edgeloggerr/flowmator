## What is Google Flow AI prompt automation?

**Google Flow AI prompt automation** is the programmatic process of submitting, monitoring, and sequencing prompts into Google Flow AI's rich-text interface without manual copy-pasting. Unlike generic browser auto-clickers, verification-driven automation inspects the active DOM state to confirm an image tile has finished rendering before advancing to the next prompt in sequence.

For AI filmmakers, storyboarders, and digital artists managing production shot lists, automated sequencing eliminates the need to constantly monitor the browser while guaranteeing that failed frames do not corrupt downstream scenes.

---

## The Challenge: Why Naive Timers Fail on Google Flow

Most creators experimenting with browser automation attempt to use fixed delay timers (such as a browser extension that waits 30 seconds between clicks). On a cloud-backed generative platform like Google Flow AI, this approach fails for three distinct reasons:

1. **Dynamic Generation Latency:** Cloud GPU render times fluctuate between 8 seconds and 90 seconds depending on global server load. A fixed 30-second timer will advance prematurely during high-traffic windows.
2. **Angular & ProseMirror State Hurdles:** Google Flow's input field is not an ordinary HTML `<textarea>`. It is an Angular-managed ProseMirror contenteditable surface that requires synthetic transaction events or `isTrusted: true` keyboard dispatching.
3. **Silent Rate Limits and Moderation Flags:** When Google Cloud triggers a temporary rate-limit or safety flag, it renders a `<flow-error-tile>`. Fixed timers blindly ignore this error tile, pushing subsequent prompts into an error state.

## The Two Core Automation Philosophies

When automating prompts in Google Flow AI, creators choose between two distinct execution models:

### 1. Safe Sequence Mode (Continuity-Oriented)

In **Safe Sequence Mode**, the automation engine takes a snapshot of the canvas before submitting each prompt:
- It records existing image `data-media-id` attributes.
- It polls the canvas every 1.5 seconds for up to 90 seconds.
- It monitors for `<flow-error-tile>` nodes.
- If a render fails or rate-limits, it triggers a 12-second cool-off delay before retrying.
- If retries fail, it halts the entire queue to protect narrative continuity.

This mode is essential for multi-shot storyboards where Shot 02 relies directly on the context of Shot 01.

### 2. Turbo Interval Mode (Throughput-Oriented)

In **Turbo Interval Mode**, prompts are injected at randomized interval cadences (such as 25s to 45s) with human-like keystroke jitter (100ms–500ms). This mode bypasses the 90-second polling loop and is ideal for broad exploratory concepting where prompt order is not strictly narrative-dependent.

## Step-by-Step: Setting Up an Automated Prompt Queue

To execute your first automated prompt queue in Google Flow AI:

### Step 1: Format Your Prompts
Prepare your prompt list in a clean text document or CSV file. Separate prompts by double line breaks or use the `@@@NEXT@@@` delimiter:

```text
01 — Wide establishing shot of a rain-slicked futuristic detective agency, midnight neon lighting, 35mm film grain

@@@NEXT@@@

02 — Medium tracking shot of the detective in a wet trenchcoat stepping out into cobblestone street, matching atmosphere
```

### Step 2: Open Google Flow AI in Chrome
Navigate to `flow.google.com` or `labs.google/flow` in Google Chrome and sign in to your Google account. Ensure the page has fully loaded and the prompt input area is visible.

### Step 3: Launch Flowmator
Open the Flowmator side panel. Confirm that the status badge indicates `● Flow Connected`. Paste your formatted prompts into the queue input box or click **Upload CSV**.

### Step 4: Configure Mode & Delays
For storyboard sequences, select **Safe Sequence Mode** with **2 Retries** and a **12-second Cool-off Delay**. For experimental bulk batches, select **Turbo Interval Mode**.

### Step 5: Start the Sequence
Click **Start Flowmator Sequence**. Monitor the real-time status transitions from *Generating* to *Verifying* to *Completed*.
