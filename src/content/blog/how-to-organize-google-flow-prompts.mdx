## How should you organize prompt queues for Google Flow AI?

**To organize prompt queues effectively for Google Flow AI**, group your prompts into structured, numbered shot sequences using consistent stylistic anchors (lighting, film stock, aspect ratio) while varying only the focal subject and camera angle. Storing these sequences in a standardized CSV format allows you to import entire script scenes directly into automated execution queues.

Unstructured prompt lists frequently produce disconnected images with clashing aesthetics. A disciplined organizational framework guarantees visual consistency across multi-shot sequences.

---

## 1. The Anatomy of a Sequential Prompt

Every prompt in a storyboard sequence should contain three distinct structural layers:

1. **The Sequence Anchor (Fixed across all shots):** Defines the art style, color grade, and environmental context. Example: `monochrome film noir, 1940s, rain streaks, 35mm grain, anamorphic lens`.
2. **The Camera Grammar (Progressive):** Explicitly specifies the lens distance and camera movement. Example: `wide establishing shot`, `medium tracking shot`, or `extreme close-up`.
3. **The Narrative Action (Dynamic):** The specific subject interaction occurring within this particular frame. Example: `detective inspecting classified telegram under desk lamp`.

By locking the Sequence Anchor and progressing the Camera Grammar, Google Flow AI maintains visual coherence between successive frames.

---

## 2. Formatting Queues Using CSV Spreadsheets

Managing dozens of prompts inside a single text document often leads to formatting mistakes. A standard CSV spreadsheet format provides clear visibility over scene IDs and prompt text.

Here is the recommended CSV format supported by Flowmator:

```csv
Index,SceneID,PromptText
1,SCENE_01_A,"1. Establishing shot, rainy detective office window at midnight, neon Venetian blinds casting shadows, monochrome film noir style"
2,SCENE_01_B,"2. Medium close-up, rugged detective in trenchcoat examining classified telegram under dim desk lamp, high contrast chiaroscuro"
3,SCENE_01_C,"3. Low-angle tracking shot, trenchcoat figure stepping out into wet reflective cobblestone street, dark alleys, dramatic backlight"
4,SCENE_01_D,"4. Climax over-the-shoulder shot, silhouette confrontation beneath street lamp with smoke rising from subway grate, 35mm film grain"
```

### Tips for CSV Formatting:
- Always wrap prompt texts containing commas in standard double quotes (`"`).
- Omit line breaks inside individual spreadsheet cells.
- Keep a master template sheet with your favorite cinematic styles.

## 3. Delimiter-Based Pasting (`@@@NEXT@@@`)

If you prefer drafting prompts inside a Markdown script editor (such as Obsidian, Notion, or Final Draft), you can separate prompts using Flowmator's custom delimiter:

```text
A panoramic aerial shot of Neo-Shinjuku 2099, holographic billboards reflecting off wet streets, cyan volumetric lighting

@@@NEXT@@@

A street-level medium shot of a cyborg courier weaving through a crowded night market on a monowheel, motion blur
```

Flowmator automatically parses this delimiter when you click **Add to Queue**, splitting the text into independent queue items with zero manual formatting needed.

## 4. In-Flight Prompt Editing

Even the best-planned prompt queues occasionally need adjustments. If Google Flow renders a composition that slightly drifts from your intended direction, you do not need to delete and recreate the queue:

- In the Flowmator side panel, failed or paused items display an inline ✏️ **Edit** button.
- Click the pencil icon to modify prompt wording directly.
- Click 🔄 **Retry** to immediately test the revised prompt without losing your place in the overall sequence.
