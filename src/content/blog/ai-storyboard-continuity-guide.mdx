## How do you maintain character and scene continuity across AI storyboards?

**To maintain continuity across AI-generated storyboard frames**, construct a locked "Visual DNA Anchor" containing fixed character features (hair, wardrobe, distinctive facial traits), environmental lighting (color temperature, time of day, lens type), and art direction, then append progressive camera angles (wide establishing, medium action, over-the-shoulder, close-up) sequentially without allowing queue gaps.

When automated without verification, a single missing frame can desynchronize an entire storyboard. Using verification-driven sequencing ensures every shot in the narrative chain is confirmed before moving forward.

---

## 1. Defining the Visual DNA Anchor

Generative image models lack persistent long-term memory between independent prompts. To force consistency across frames, you must carry forward the exact descriptive tokens that define your scene's visual grammar:

### Example Visual DNA Template:
- **Protagonist:** `30-year-old female cyber-operative, asymmetric silver bob haircut, high-collar matte black trenchcoat, glowing cyan ocular implant on right eye`
- **Environment:** `rain-drenched dystopian Neo-Shinjuku alley, wet asphalt reflections, magenta and cyan holographic neon signage`
- **Cinematography:** `anamorphic lens, shallow depth of field, 35mm motion picture film stock, cinematic chiaroscuro`

Combine these elements into a single anchor block. In every subsequent prompt in the scene, paste this anchor block verbatim, modifying only the camera distance and character action.

---

## 2. The 4-Shot Cinematic Progression Framework

A classic storyboard sequence moves the audience through a visual narrative arc. Here is how to structure a 4-shot progression for Google Flow AI:

### Shot 01: The Establishing Wide Shot
Sets the geographic scale and environmental mood:
```text
1. Wide establishing cinematic shot of rain-drenched dystopian Neo-Shinjuku alley, towering holographic skyscrapers, a lone figure in black trenchcoat standing beneath flickering street lamp, 35mm grain
```

### Shot 02: Medium Action Shot
Introduces the character's movement and relationship to the space:
```text
2. Medium shot of the same female operative in black trenchcoat walking briskly down the wet cobblestones, silver bob haircut caught in wind, neon light reflecting off cyan ocular implant
```

### Shot 03: Close-Up Emotion Shot
Focuses on intent, dialogue, or tension:
```text
3. Close-up portrait of the female operative ducking under a neon canopy, examining a glowing holographic datapad, raindrops running down cheek, intense expression, cyan eye glow illuminating face
```

### Shot 04: Climax / Reverse Angle Shot
Resolves the scene or transitions to the next beat:
```text
4. Over-the-shoulder tracking shot behind the female operative, looking down the dark alleyway toward an approaching armored vehicle with glaring headlights cutting through the steam
```

If Shot 02 fails due to a temporary moderation trigger or rate limit, and a naive script advances to Shot 03 without it:

- The action sequence jumps abruptly.
